# PO016: Cloud flow has more than 50 actions or more than 4 levels of nesting

Severity: warning

## What it finds

A cloud flow with more than 50 actions, or with more than 4 levels of nesting. The message gives both counts, so a baselined finding comes back when the flow's size or depth changes.

Every action under the flow's `actions` counts. That includes the actions inside a Scope, both branches of a Condition, every case and the default of a Switch, and a loop. A container counts as an action itself. The trigger doesn't count.

Depth is the number of containers around the deepest action. An action at the top is at depth 0, one inside a Scope is at depth 1, and one inside a Condition inside a Scope is at depth 2. An action in a Switch case is one level below the Switch, as an action in a Condition branch is one level below the Condition: an action in a case of a Switch inside a Scope is at depth 2, not 3.

Learn doesn't say how its limits count nested actions or switch cases, so this counting is the rule's own reading.

## Why it matters

Learn says flows with a high number of actions or deeply nested scopes might experience slower performance in the designer and during runs, and that a flow with many actions can be slow to edit even below 500. Learn's limits are 500 actions per flow and 8 levels of nesting (scopes, conditions, switch cases and loops). A flow over 8 levels doesn't save or run.

The 50 and the 4 are not product limits. They are the review heuristic of Microsoft's CAT OverFlow skill (MIT, microsoft/power-cat-skills at commit f898c77), which rates "total action count over 50, or nesting depth over 4" as high impact. It walks the same branches as this rule, but gives no reason and doesn't define depth. Learn gives no lower number.

## How to fix

Move self-contained parts of the flow into child flows. Learn's limits page suggests child flows to reduce the actions in one flow. Flatten conditions that only guard the next one.

Or keep the flow, and baseline the finding as Suppress says.

## Suppress

To accept the finding for now, run `powerops baseline` and use `powerops analyze --baseline powerops-baseline.json`. A baselined finding is matched on solution, rule, component and message.

Because the message holds both counts, a baselined PO016 finding, or a dismissed code-scanning alert for it, comes back when the flow's action count or depth changes.
